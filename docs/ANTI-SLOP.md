# Anti-generic-deck baseline

The universal deck-building floor. It applies to **every pilot and every commander**, before
a single card is suggested, and the pilot's mined `profile/playstyle.md` layers on top.
The goal: a deck that feels intentionally built, not assembled from an EDHREC average.

## Avoid these patterns

- **Blind EDHREC average** — including a card because it's in 60% of lists without knowing
  why it's there. Every card needs a declared role (its package). "It's a staple" is not a role.
- **Goodstuff piles** — collections of individually powerful cards with no coherent plan.
  Powerful cards are fine; powerful cards with no synergy are a 5 masquerading as an 8.
- **Unfocused threat density** — too many "scary" cards in too many directions, so the deck
  never threatens anything specific. Choose a lane, then be good at that lane.
- **Under-powered win conditions** — 40-land draw-go with no plan to actually win. "I'll
  draw into a win eventually" is not a win condition.
- **Over-represented removal** — 15 removal spells and 3 win conditions means you answer
  everything and threaten nothing. Balance the matrix.
- **Curve ignorance** — a 3.8 average CMC deck with 8 ramp spells will consistently miss
  its commander by turns 2-3. Count the curve honestly.

## Prefer

- **Named purpose for every slot** — each card has a package and a reason. If you can't
  name why it's in this deck specifically (not just "it's a good card"), it probably
  doesn't belong.
- **Redundancy in key packages** — if the strategy requires drawing cards, include 10 draw
  effects, not 4. The deck should still function when 2-3 draw pieces are answered.
- **Honest power level alignment** — build for the power level the pilot can actually pilot
  and the table the deck will play at. A 9-rated deck at a casual table is not fun for anyone.
- **Specific over generic** — "makes tokens when lands enter" is a better fit for Aesi than
  "is generically powerful." The commander defines the lane; the deck should drive it.
